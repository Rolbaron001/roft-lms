## 4. Storage, backups and the value chain

This is the largest item and it has a deadline.

### 4.1 Backup retention
**Status:** done, 21 September. **Needs:** confirming to Heidi.

The disk reached 93 per cent. The cause was not the application data, which is
a little over 1 GB. It was 8.5 GB of backups, of which everything older than
seven days accounted for 0.08 GB.

Two fixes went in on 21 September:

* The pre deploy backup now takes the database only. The evidence archive, a
  full copy of every uploaded file at roughly 1 GB, is taken nightly instead of
  before every deploy.
* Evidence archives are kept by count, the newest three, rather than by age.
  Age released nothing because all the weight was recent.

A third fix followed the same day. Docker images were also kept by age at 72
hours, and six deploys in one day all sat inside that window, so the prune
reclaimed nothing while 4.6 GB accumulated. Images are now kept by count, the
newest two versions, as app and tools pairs.

Result: 93 per cent full down to 60 per cent, 7.5 GB free.

Heidi's own summary of the underlying problem is correct and worth keeping:
the evidence archive is the cost, not the database dump, and both halves are
required or a restored Portfolio of Evidence points at files that no longer
exist.

### 5.3 Explain and test the blueprint route
**Owner:** Claude to explain, Roland to test. **Status:** explained 22
September; the test is waiting on sandbox access.

A folder carrying `_control/blueprint.json` is read directly, with no model, no
token and no quota, and gives the same result every time. The platform can now
write one for a qualification it already holds, so the expensive route becomes
a one off.

**Why it matters commercially.** The uploads for the first ten clients each
cost an afternoon today. With a blueprint, each qualification is a solved
problem the second time it is loaded: on another tenant, on a second site,
after a restore, or when the client loads it themselves. It is the difference
between ten uploads and ten first uploads. It does not help a qualification
nobody has loaded yet, so the first import is still the parser or the AI route.

**The three ways the platform can learn a structure**, measured on 121151:

| Route | Lines read | Cost | Repeatable |
|---|---|---|---|
| The curriculum parser | 503 of 503 | free | yes |
| An AI extension reading the folder | 331 of 503 | tokens | varies per run |
| The folder's own blueprint | exact | free | identical every time |

**The test, end to end.**

1. On `lms.curiosa.academy`, open the HRM Officer qualification and find **Save
   its blueprint**. Press **Download 121151-blueprint.json**. Read the *What the
   file does not carry* list underneath, which is the honest part of the test.
2. Build a folder on your own machine:

   ```
   121151 HRM Officer/
     _control/
       blueprint.json
   ```

   Rename the downloaded file to `blueprint.json`. The reader looks for
   `_control/blueprint.json` exactly.
3. On `lms.roftbusiness.org`, go to `/qualifications?view=add&how=folder` and
   pick the `121151 HRM Officer` folder, not the `_control` folder inside it.
4. The proposal should say the structure was read from the folder's own
   blueprint and that nothing in it was inferred, with 15 modules. Commit it.

**What it deliberately does not carry**, reported on screen per qualification
rather than left to be discovered: the study units, because a curriculum
publishes modules and says nothing about how a provider groups them; the
documents, because a blueprint is structure rather than files; and the element
kinds, because one list per topic cannot distinguish applied knowledge from a

## 6. Tenant flexibility

**Decision:** Roland, 21 September afternoon. **Status:** built, awaiting
deploy.

The platform must fit clients who are not Curiosa: not all in South Africa,
and not all seeking accreditation. A corporate client may want a course for
employees and nothing else. A corporate client may equally be accredited to
offer a qualification, as ROFT's prospective Ranger clients would be. The
choice is therefore about what a tenant does, never about what kind of
organisation it is.

This began as five independent switches. Roland corrected it the same
afternoon: "We have already determined that Course and Study Unit are the same
thing in terms of where they rank. What the selection needs to do is to
determine how the users view them." Independent switches allowed platforms that
cannot exist, such as study units with no qualification to sit inside.

**Two questions, each with one answer.**

*What sits at the top:*

| Choice | What it means |
|---|---|
| Qualification / Programme | One thing. A learner is put onto the qualification and that is what they work through. Curiosa's shape. |
| Qualification and Programme, separately | Two different things. |
| Programmes only | No accreditation record. |
| Neither | Everything stands on its own. |

*What a learner works through:*

| Choice | What it means |
|---|---|
| Courses | The course is the thing. |
| Study units | The study unit replaces the course. Requires a qualification above it, and is unselectable without one. |

*Two switches that stand on their own:* statutory reporting, workplace
experience.

Three consequences follow from the choices rather than being set twice: the
menu and the pages behind it, the shape diagram that redraws live, and the
terminology screen, which stops offering the word a choice has already settled.

### 6.1 The QCTO wording was wrong and is corrected
Roland asked, before any of it was wired, that his references to the QCTO be
checked. They did not survive the check, and neither did mine.

Counted in the project's own documents, **"study unit" appears zero times** in
the 121151 curriculum, its qualification document, its assessment
specification, the 118709 curriculum and SAQA's NQFpedia. "Course" appears zero
times in the four QCTO documents. The regulator's word for this layer is
**learning programme**, and SAQA keeps it distinct from a qualification: a
learning programme *leads to* one.

No screen now attributes either word to the QCTO. A test asserts that no label
or description in the choices contains "QCTO" or "SAQA".

The same check corrects the working paper of 28 August, which said a study unit
is "a curriculum grouping fixed by the registered qualification itself, for
example SU1 to SU5". It is not. SU1 to SU5 are Curiosa's own grouping, which is
what the code has said all along.

### 6.2 Retire "Learning paths"

## 9. Reading Word documents correctly

**Found by:** Roland, 21 September. **Status:** fixed, awaiting deploy.

"I think the problem is that you are not reading strikethrough text
correctly." Struck through text was being read as live text.

This is not only a job sheet problem. The same reader runs over every Word
document a provider uploads: the curriculum it transcribes assessment criteria
from, the alignment document it builds study units from, and the workbooks and
answer guides Capture turns into questions a learner answers.

A criterion struck out because it was withdrawn would have been transcribed as
one learners must meet. A question struck out before a cohort sat the paper
would have been captured and asked. A superseded answer struck through beside
its replacement would have been read as the correct one. None of those would
look wrong on any screen.

Fixed in `lib/office.ts`, with `tests/docx-strikethrough.test.ts` covering both
strikethrough forms, the case where a run switches it off, and this job sheet
itself.
