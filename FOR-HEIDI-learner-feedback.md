# The "feedback questions" — asked again, more plainly

**16 September 2026. For Roland to send on to Heidi if it is useful.**

Heidi's reply to the last list was: *"I do not understand the 'feedback
questions' part of the message."* That is a fair response to how it was put —
it was asked in the platform's language rather than in hers. Here it is again.

## What is being asked about

At the end of a programme, most providers ask the learners what they thought of
it: whether the facilitator explained things clearly, whether the materials
were useful, whether the pace suited them. Curiosa does this too — the
understanding here is that it is done on a **Google Form**.

The platform can do that same job, so that the answers sit with the cohort they
belong to instead of in a separate spreadsheet, and so a report can be produced
without anybody copying anything across.

**The question is simply: which questions should it ask?**

## What it is doing in the meantime

It is not waiting. Every tenant gets a starter set of seven questions, and
Curiosa is using those now. Five are rated, two are free text:

1. The facilitator explained the material clearly. *(rating)*
2. The workbooks and materials were useful. *(rating)*
3. The pace suited me. *(rating)*
4. I can use what I learned in my job. *(rating)*
5. The assessment was a fair test of what was taught. *(rating)*
6. What worked well? *(free text)*
7. What would you change? *(free text)*

These were written by us. They are reasonable, and they are **not Curiosa's**.
Nobody should mistake them for something Curiosa approved.

## What we would like

A copy of the Google Form, or just the list of questions from it — however it
is easiest to send. A screenshot is fine.

## What happens when it arrives

The questions are replaced with Curiosa's, and that is the whole job. It is a
setting, not a change to the software, so it takes minutes and needs no
release.

## What changes if it never arrives

Nothing breaks. The seven questions above carry on being asked, and the results
carry on being collected and reported. The only cost is that the platform is
asking Curiosa's learners questions Curiosa did not write — which is worth
correcting at some point, but is not urgent and is not holding anything up.

## Two things this is *not*

- It is not the **assessment** of a learner. Nothing here is marked, and no
  answer affects anybody's result.
- It is not a **QCTO requirement** we are chasing. It is quality-of-delivery
  feedback, which is Curiosa's own business.
